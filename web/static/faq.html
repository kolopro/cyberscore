Frequently asked questions

Everything you need to know about CyberScore — how it works, how to read results, and how to improve your score.

General

Is CyberScore free? ▼

Yes. Scanning a domain, URL or email headers is completely free and requires no account, no sign-up and no credit card. There are no hidden limits on the number of scans.

Do I need to install anything? ▼

No. CyberScore runs entirely in the cloud. Enter a domain or URL in the scanner and results appear in your browser — nothing to download or configure.

Who built CyberScore? ▼

CyberScore is built by VortiSec, a security-focused software company. It is designed for website owners, developers and security professionals who want a quick, honest assessment of a site's publicly visible security posture. Read more →

What languages does CyberScore support? ▼

The scanner is available in English at cyberscore.cc and in Greek at cyberscore.cc/el. The guides and documentation are currently in English.

Scanning & results

What does CyberScore actually check? ▼

CyberScore runs five independent checks:

  • HTTPS & TLS — protocol versions, cipher suites, certificate validity and forward secrecy.
  • HTTP security headers — HSTS, Content-Security-Policy, X-Frame-Options, X-Content-Type-Options, Referrer-Policy and Permissions-Policy.
  • DNS configuration — CAA records, name-server consistency.
  • Email authentication — SPF, DKIM and DMARC records.
  • Technology detection — software and version disclosure in headers.

The URL check and email-header analyser are separate tools available from the main scanner page.

Is it safe and legal to scan any website? ▼

CyberScore performs passive checks only — it reads publicly available information using the same DNS queries and HTTP requests any browser would make. It never sends attack payloads, never probes internal infrastructure and never accesses anything that is not publicly visible. This makes it safe and legal to use on any public website.

As a matter of good practice, scan only sites you own or have been authorised to test.

Does CyberScore store my scan results? ▼

No. Results are computed on demand and returned to your browser. CyberScore does not store scan results associated with your identity, build a history of scanned domains or share data with third parties beyond what is needed for reputation lookups (the domain or URL is sent to reputation providers to check against their databases). See our Privacy Policy for the full details.

How long does a scan take? ▼

A standard scan typically completes in 5–15 seconds. The five checking modules (HTTPS, headers, DNS, email, technology) run in parallel, so the total time is roughly that of the slowest module. The Deep TLS scan takes a little longer — usually 15–30 seconds — because it performs a more thorough TLS protocol assessment.

Can I scan an IP address instead of a domain? ▼

The main scanner is optimised for domain names and will extract the hostname from whatever you enter (including full URLs like https://example.com/page). Raw IP addresses can be entered but some checks (like DNS and email authentication) will return no results because they rely on domain names. For the URL analyser, raw IPs are accepted and flagged as a warning signal.

Understanding your score

How is the score calculated? ▼

The score starts at 100. Each finding deducts points based on its severity:

  • HIGH — deducts 20 points (e.g. no HTTPS, missing SPF).
  • MEDIUM — deducts 10 points (e.g. missing HSTS, no DMARC enforcement).
  • LOW — deducts 3 points, capped at 20 total from low findings.

The score cannot go below 0. Read the full scoring guide →

What does each grade mean? ▼
  • A (90–100) — excellent. All major controls are in place.
  • B (75–89) — good. A few best-practice gaps remain.
  • C (55–74) — fair. Several medium issues need attention.
  • D (35–54) — poor. Meaningful security risks are present.
  • F (0–34) — failing. Critical gaps that should be fixed immediately.
My site scored an F. Where do I start? ▼

Open the Remediation tab in your scan results. All findings are sorted by severity (highest first) with copy-ready configuration snippets. Fix HIGH issues first — each one is worth 20 points and usually involves enabling HTTPS or adding email authentication records. MEDIUM issues (10 points each) are typically missing security headers, which can be added in a few lines of server configuration.

Our common vulnerabilities guide covers the most frequent findings with step-by-step fixes.

Does a high score mean my site is secure? ▼

A high score means the common, automatable baseline controls are in place — HTTPS is configured correctly, security headers are present, email is authenticated. It does not mean the application is free of SQL injection, that access controls are correct or that there are no logic vulnerabilities. Those require manual testing or a penetration test. Think of a CyberScore result as a hygiene baseline: necessary but not sufficient on its own.

Email & URL tools

How does the email header analyser work? ▼

Paste the full raw headers from a suspicious email (available via "Show original", "View source" or similar in your mail client). CyberScore parses the Authentication-Results header to read SPF, DKIM and DMARC pass/fail verdicts, checks for mismatches between the From address, Return-Path and Reply-To, and identifies common spoofing signals. No email is sent — only the headers you paste are analysed.

What does the URL checker look for? ▼

The URL checker inspects the structure of a URL for phishing signals: dangerous schemes (javascript:, data:), embedded credentials (paypal.com@evil.tld tricks), raw IP addresses, punycode look-alike domains, risky TLDs, URL shorteners and credential-harvesting keywords. It also follows the redirect chain once to see the final destination, and checks the URL against multi-engine reputation databases (URLhaus, Google Safe Browsing, VirusTotal where keys are configured).
